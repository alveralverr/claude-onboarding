## `cowork-task-runs` — How a Cowork task runs

> A Cowork task is really three moves. First, you delegate — send a prompt that says what you need and what "done" looks like. Then Claude takes it from there: it clarifies the goal, plans the steps right in the sidebar, works across your files, tools, and the web, checks its own output, and delivers finished files with a summary. And third, you simply come back to completed work — files in your folder, actions taken in your tools, and a record of everything that happened. You stay in control the whole way: every action shows up in the sidebar, and Claude pauses for your okay before anything consequential. Picture handing it a stack of receipts and getting back a clean spreadsheet, or dropping in your templates and getting polished docs that even refresh on a schedule. The better you set it up, the better it delivers. But before we talk about briefing it well, there's one quiet thing that shapes every single task — Claude's memory.

## `context-window` — Context Window

> Here's a concept that'll save you a lot of frustration: the context window. Think of it as Claude's working memory — a whiteboard it can see all at once. Everything lands on that board: your prompts, Claude's replies, the files you attach, every tool call. The catch? The board is a fixed size, and it doesn't grow mid-conversation. Watch the animation and you'll see exactly why that matters — with each turn, the whole history carries forward and stacks higher and higher, until it reaches the edge. And once it's full, the oldest pieces fall off and get cut off. When that happens, Claude starts losing track of what you told it earlier, and replies get shorter, repetitive, or miss details you already gave. The fix is easy: start a fresh session for each new task, don't chain unrelated work into one long thread, and if something genuinely needs to carry over, just paste a quick summary into the new chat. Keep that board clear, and Claude stays sharp. Now, memory is just the canvas — Claude also comes with tools that do a lot of the work for you. Let's start with Skills.

## `connectors` — Connectors

> If skills are the know-how, connectors are the keys. They let Claude work directly inside the apps you already use — Gmail, Calendar, Drive, Notion, Slack — reading your data and taking action there on your behalf, with no copy-pasting between tabs. Set one up once, and it's available in every conversation from then on. One thing to keep in mind: skills and connectors work hand in hand, so a skill that relies on a connector you haven't added yet won't run — but don't worry, Claude will flag it so you know exactly what to switch on. Watch the videos here to picture how connectors slot into your real, day-to-day tasks. Wire these up, and Claude plugs right into the way you already work. And to get the best out of all of it, it comes down to how you ask — so let's talk prompting.

## `prompting` — Prompting for Cowork

> This is the single most useful thing I can tell you: Claude is brilliant on day one — it just doesn't know *your* situation yet. It knows every language and format under the sun, but not your client, your task, or what "good" looks like to you. So give it that. Attach the file, paste the email, add the context. If the output ever misses, it's almost always missing context — not a Claude problem, so feed it more instead of agonizing over perfect wording. One more setting worth getting right: which model you're on.

## `safety` — Use Claude Cowork safely

> Please read this before any client work. Cowork has real access to your files, your browser, and your connected apps — and that power cuts both ways. So steer it away from sensitive things like financial docs and passwords, be extra careful when it's controlling your screen, and keep a human in the loop for anything risky or irreversible, like logins, payments, or deletions.
