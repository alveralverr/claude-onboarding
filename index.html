Meet Claude Cowork

Less chatbot, more like a colleague.

Cowork finishes tasks end to end. It reads your files, works through each step, and saves the finished result. Think of a coworker who can think, click, and get things done.

  • Uses your local folders. Reads and writes files on your computer. No uploading, and output saves next to your inputs.
  • Runs parallel work. Splits multi-part tasks and runs the independent pieces at the same time.
  • Runs on a schedule. Set a cadence once and recurring work runs by itself.

Step 3 of 3 · about 5 minutes

Use Cowork safely.

Cowork works on your computer with access to your files, browser, connected services, and apps. Read this, then pass the short check before any client work.

To keep risk low

  • Don't give Claude access to local files with sensitive information, such as financial documents and passwords.
  • Be especially careful with computer use. When you allow it, Claude makes changes on your computer directly and skips permission checks.
  • Cowork may have access to Claude in Chrome. Be extremely careful with anything that takes control of your screen. Don't automate risky or irreversible actions such as logins, payments, or deletions.

Six habits that protect you and your client.

  1. Be selective about file access. Claude can read, write, and delete any file it can reach. Use a dedicated working folder and keep backups of anything important.
  2. Watch the task, not every command. Look for surprises: files or sites you didn't mention, or scope creeping beyond your ask. If something feels off, stop the task.
  3. Be cautious with scheduled tasks. They run without you watching. Start low-risk, don't schedule messages, purchases, or hard-to-undo actions, and review results often.
  4. Be cautious with computer use. Avoid anything that takes over your screen, apps, or browser.
  5. Skip unfamiliar plugins and skills. Stick to Anthropic-verified connectors from the desktop directory, and review permissions when you install.
  6. Take care with Claude add-ins in other apps. Claude can share data across apps like Excel and PowerPoint without being told to. Avoid sensitive information while Cowork is active.

Magic rules

  • Never paste passwords, API keys, or credentials into a Cowork prompt. Don't ask it to reach private data or scrape personal information, especially your client's.
  • Never connect a client's email, calendar, or cloud storage without their explicit approval.
  • Never impersonate someone. Ask instead: "Based on their communication style, how would they respond?"
  • Don't change security settings directly. Claude will refuse.
  • Don't use Claude for clients whose confidentiality clauses cover AI tools. Ask your Account Lead first.
  • Aerospace, defense, or government clients: don't connect any client accounts. Personal productivity only.
  • Always review everything before it goes to a client. Your sign-off is the last check.

You stay responsible for

  • Content published and messages sent.
  • Purchases or financial transactions.
  • Data accessed or changed.
  • Actions taken by scheduled tasks on your behalf.
  • Actions taken through computer use on your desktop and apps.
  • Respecting third-party terms of service, including limits on automated access.

Anthropic has built in safety measures, but you're still responsible for how you use Claude. Report suspicious behaviour or critical issues to product-team@getmagicea.com. Not sure if something is allowed? Ask your Account Lead.

How Cowork works.

Cowork is the mode in Claude desktop that runs on your computer and completes work across your files, browser, and tools. As a Magic assistant, make Cowork your default mode.

Chat

You drive every step

You ask, follow up, and refine, one reply at a time. Then you assemble the output yourself.

Cowork

You delegate, it delivers

You give one goal. It reads files, searches Drive, and drafts in parallel, then saves finished work to your folder.

Always use Cowork when

  • You have a clear deliverable or outcome.
  • The work touches your files or connected tools.
  • You'd rather hand off the job and review the output.
  • You're running recurring tasks on a schedule.

How a task runs.

  1. You delegate the work. Describe the goal: what you need done and what done looks like.
  2. Claude understands. It asks clarifying questions to be sure of your goal.
  3. Claude plans. It breaks the work into steps you can see in the sidebar.
  4. Claude executes. It works across your files, tools, and the web.
  5. Claude verifies. It checks its own output for quality and accuracy.
  6. You come back to finished work. Files in your folder, actions taken in your tools, and a summary of what happened.

Every action shows in the sidebar. Claude pauses and asks for your approval before doing anything consequential in your working folder.

Claude's working memory has a limit.

Every session holds a fixed amount of input, called the context window. When it fills, older context drops off and quality slips.

What it is

Everything Claude can see in a session: your prompts, its replies, attached files, and tool calls. It has a fixed size and doesn't grow mid-session.

When it fills

Claude loses sight of earlier instructions. Replies get shorter, repeat themselves, or miss details you already gave.

How to manage it

Start a fresh session for each new task. Don't chain unrelated work in one conversation. Paste a short summary if context needs to carry over.

Scheduled tasks run themselves.

Type /schedule in Cowork. Claude asks what, when, and how often, and confirms before creating anything. Define it once and it takes it from there.

Every weekday morning at 8am, check my inbox and save a summary of unread client emails to my folder.

Every Friday at 4pm, pull my completed tasks for the week and create a short summary for my AL.

Every Monday, check my client's calendar for the week and flag any conflicts or gaps in scheduling.

Scheduled tasks run while you're not watching. Start with low-risk tasks and don't schedule messages, purchases, or hard-to-undo actions. See safety.

Give Claude the context.

Context does the heavy lifting. Add your files, attach screenshots, paste the email, link connector data, or call a skill. Then say what you need out of it.

The number one rule

Claude is brilliant on day one. It just doesn't know your situation yet.

It knows every language, industry, and format. It doesn't know your client, your task, or what a good result looks like. The more relevant material you hand over, the less it has to guess. If the output missed, it's almost always a context problem. Add information instead of polishing the wording.

Polished prompt, no context

"Analyze our sales data and turn it into a slide deck with charts. Keep it accurate and clear."

The context, handed over

"Go to my Q2 Reports folder and open regional_sales.csv. Read every row, analyze the trends, and turn the key insights into charts for the quarterly business review. Build it into a slide deck, and double-check every figure against the sheet before you finalize."

Three things to focus on.

  1. Link and feed the raw data. Connect apps and folders, attach files, paste the email, share screenshots, then say what you need. What you hand over matters far more than how you word it.
  2. Refine the first draft if needed. If it isn't right, add context and redirect, the way you would with a colleague who needed more of the story.
  3. Assume it can do it until it says otherwise. Try the task. Claude flags what's out of reach and asks questions when it needs more. The people who get the most out of it keep asking.

A screenshot of the thread, two skills, and your voice guide. Show Claude the history instead of retyping it.

A hands-off recurring task only works because you named the exact source, destination, and people. Set the context once and it runs every week.

A template, your pricing, the discovery notes, their deck, and the format you want out. Your files do the heavy lifting.

Too generic?The output exists. It just needs more to go on.

"That's too generic. This is for a healthcare client preparing a board update. Lead with the compliance risks from our Q2 deck, frame them against the targets in our KPI sheet, and keep it to the three slides that matter."

Audience, two source files, and a sharper frame. You didn't rephrase the ask. You added what Claude couldn't see.

Cowork tips

  • State the output format up front: "save as a .md file", "create a table", "write bullet points".
  • State the output location: "save to my Magic Work folder in Google Drive".
  • For inbox or calendar tasks, set the scope: "only emails from the last 48 hours" or "just the three meetings on Thursday".
  • Don't interrupt mid-step unless it's clearly going wrong. Wait for a result, then redirect.
  • You don't need polished prompts. Voice-to-text, Taglish, abbreviations, and typos are fine. Worry less about phrasing and more about whether Claude has what it needs.
  • Start a new chat when you switch topics. Long conversations that jump between subjects confuse Claude.

Things that trip people up

  • Claude can hit usage limits. "Resets at 5am" means your cap was reached. Switch to a simpler task or step down to Sonnet.
  • If Claude goes quiet mid-task, it may be waiting on a permission prompt. Check the task sidebar before restarting.
  • Confirm the project folder, file path, skills, and connectors before a task runs, so Claude reads and saves exactly where you intend.
  • If Claude goes off track, don't reword the same ask. Redirect it with the right context. Generic or wrong output usually means Claude couldn't see what you could.
