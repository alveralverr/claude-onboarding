What's different about Claude

Claude does things, not just answers requests.

You may have used MagicGPT and ChatGPT. Claude sits in the same place — you chat, it answers — with one difference that changes how you'll work day to day.

Claude Cowork runs on your own machine, with your real files and tools. ChatGPT can act too, but inside a capped cloud sandbox built for consumer tasks. Cowork is built for the work you actually do, with pre-built Magic skill templates ready from day one.

Introducing Claude Cowork

Less chatbot, more like a colleague.

Cowork completes tasks end-to-end — reading your files, working through each step, and saving finished results. Think of it as a coworker who can think, click, and get things done.

  • Access your local folders Reads and writes files directly on your computer. No uploading — output saves next to your inputs.
  • Delegate parallel work Splits multi-part tasks and runs the independent pieces simultaneously.
  • Run tasks on a schedule Set a cadence once — recurring work runs automatically.

Installation

Getting started with Cowork

Connect your project folder

A folder

A folder on your computer. Cowork reads what's there and saves new files alongside it. Scope it to one piece of work, or point it at a broader directory several tasks share.

A project

A workspace that carries its own files, instructions, and memory across sessions. Create one in Cowork's sidebar, or import a project you already use in Chat.

Keep the desktop app open while you work. Cowork only runs through the desktop app, not the browser. The folder you select is Claude's workspace where it can read, write, or delete files.

Step three

~6 min

Connect your apps.

Claude connects to popular apps — Microsoft 365, Notion, Slack, Canva, Asana, HubSpot, QuickBooks, and many more — so you can bring agentic AI into your real workflows. Cowork's real power comes from connecting the apps your client already uses.

Test it

If you're connected to your calendar, open a new Cowork session and type:

What meetings do I have tomorrow?

If Claude lists your calendar events, you're connected. If it says it doesn't have access, go back to Connectors and check.

⚠️ Connect your own Google account only. Never connect a client's email or calendar without their explicit permission.

Step five

~10 min

Try your first Cowork task.

Open a new Cowork session and start on something from your to-do list — a task that's spread across your tools, takes several steps, or needs real work on your computer.

How to run it

  1. In Cowork, set the working folder to send with your prompt.
  2. Type clear, specific instructions describing what you need.
  3. Claude may show its plan or ask clarifying questions before anything consequential. If it's right, approve it. If not, redirect it.
  4. Watch Claude work through the steps, or step away and come back.
  5. Always review Claude's output before sending anything to a client.
  6. If the output needs work, re-prompt until you get the outcome you want.

Sample first tasks

Make sure you have linked the appropriate folders, files, connectors, or skills. Always think "what does the AI need to know".

Go through my inbox, find unread client emails from the last 24 hours, and draft a reply for each one that needs a response. Save the drafts to my Magic Work folder. Check my Google Calendar for next week and write a summary of my client's availability. Save it as availability.md in my folder. Research [company name] — their industry, recent news, and key people. Write a one-page briefing and save it to my folder. Summarize these meeting notes and pull out every action item with the owner and deadline.

To get more practice, try doing tasks in Cowork using the connectors or skills for your real-world tasks.

Using Claude Cowork

The mode where Claude stops answering and starts doing.

Cowork is the agentic mode in Claude Desktop — it runs on your computer and completes work across your files, browser, and tools. As a Magic assistant, always use Cowork as your default mode.

Always use Cowork

Your job is task execution. Cowork is how Claude executes.

Use it whenever:

  • You have a clear deliverable or outcome.
  • The work touches your files or connected tools.
  • You'd rather hand off the job and review the output.
  • You're running recurring tasks on a schedule.

How a Cowork task runs

Delegate, but stay in control.

Every action appears in the sidebar. Claude pauses and asks for your approval before doing anything consequential on your working folder.

Hand Claude a pile of receipts, invoices, or screenshots. Come back to a formatted spreadsheet with all the data extracted and organized.

Give Claude your templates and source materials. It produces polished docs, decks, or reports — and can run on a schedule to keep them current.

Context Window

Claude's working memory. It has a limit.

Every session holds a fixed amount of input. When it fills, older context drops off and quality degrades.

What it is

A rolling window of everything Claude can see — your prompts, its replies, attached files, tool calls. Fixed in size and doesn't grow mid-session.

When it fills

Claude loses sight of earlier instructions and context. Responses become shorter, repetitive, or miss details you've already given.

How to manage it

Start a fresh session for each new task. Don't chain unrelated work in one conversation. Paste a brief summary if context needs to carry over.

Context window, extended thinking, and tool access — how they interact in a Cowork session.

Scheduled tasks

Have Claude run work on a cadence — without you starting it.

Type /schedule in Cowork. Claude asks a few questions — what, when, how often — and confirms before anything is created. You define it once and it handles it from there.

Sample prompts for scheduled tasks

Every weekday morning at 8am, check my inbox and save a summary of unread client emails to my folder. Every Friday at 4pm, pull my completed tasks for the week and create a short summary for my AL. Every Monday, check my client's calendar for the week and flag any conflicts or gaps in scheduling.

Prompting for Cowork

Tell it what you want delivered, not what you want to know.

Two things get you something usable: share the situation — attach the file, screenshot the thread, paste the email — and say what you want out of it. The wording is important but the context you provide matters the most.

The #1 rule

Claude is brilliant on day one. It just doesn't know your situation yet.

It knows every language, industry, and format. What it doesn't know is your client, your task, and what a good result looks like. Give it that context — the more you hand over, the less it has to guess.

If the output wasn't what you wanted, that is almost always a context problem — not a Claude problem.

Polished prompt, nothing shared

"Analyze our sales data and turn it into a slide deck with charts. Keep it accurate and clear."

The file, pointed to

"Go to my Q2 Reports folder and open regional_sales.csv. Read every row, analyze the trends, and turn the key insights into charts for the quarterly business review. Build it into a slide deck — and double-check every figure against the sheet before you finalize."

Three things to focus on

01

Link and feed the raw materials.

Connect your apps and folders, attach files, paste the email, share screenshots or other data. What you hand over matters far more than how you word the ask.

02

Refine the first draft if needed.

Claude iterates. If the result isn't right, redirect it — just like you would with a colleague.

03

Assume it can do anything until it tells you otherwise.

Try the task. Claude will flag it if something is outside what it can do. Most assistants stop short of what is actually possible — the people who get the most out of it are the ones who keep asking.

Cowork-specific tips

  • State the output format upfront —
    "save as a .md file", "create a table", "write bullet points".
  • State the output location —
    "save to my Magic Work folder in Google Drive".
  • For tasks touching your inbox or calendar, define the scope —
    "only emails from the last 48 hours" or "just the three meetings on Thursday".
  • Claude makes changes in real-time. Don't interrupt mid-step unless it is clearly going wrong. Wait for Claude to surface a result, then redirect.
  • There's no one-size-fits-all prompt anatomy that works for everyone due to the wide range of tasks and goals. Given the right context, Claude even understands voice-to-text, Taglish, abbreviations, or half-finished sentences. Claude needs to understand the situation more than receiving polished wording.

Things that trip people up

  • Claude can hit rate limits — "reset at 5am" means your usage cap was reached. Switch to a simpler task or step down to Sonnet.
  • If Claude goes quiet mid-task, it may be waiting on a permission prompt — check the task sidebar before restarting.
  • Always confirm the folder or file path before a task runs — check that the right project folder, sub-folder, or file location is selected so Claude saves or reads from exactly where you intend.

Use Claude Cowork safely

Read this before any client work.

Cowork works on your computer with access to your files, browser, connected services, and apps. That capability comes with risks worth understanding.

To minimize risks

  • Avoid granting access to local files with sensitive information, like financial documents and passwords.
  • Be especially cautious with computer use — Claude makes changes on your computer directly and bypass permission checks when you allow it to.
  • Cowork may have direct access to Claude in Chrome. We strongly advise against using anything that takes control over your screen since it's a massive risk and wastes tokens. Keep everything in Claude itself.

Responsible Use

Understand Anthropic's safety measures — what they've built, what to watch for, and how to protect yourself and your client.

1

Be selective about file access.

Claude can read, write, and delete any files it has access to. Avoid pointing it at sensitive documents — use a dedicated working folder instead, and keep backups of anything important.

2

Monitor tasks, not just commands.

Watch out for unexpected patterns rather than validating every command — is Claude accessing files or sites you didn't mention? Is scope creeping beyond what you asked for? If something feels off, stop the task immediately.

3

Be cautious with scheduled tasks.

Scheduled tasks run without you watching. Start with low-risk tasks, avoid scheduling anything involving messages, purchases, or hard-to-undo actions, and review results regularly.

4

Be cautious with computer use.

Avoid anything that interacts directly with your screen, apps, and browser.

5

Avoid unfamiliar third-party plugins or skills.

Stick to Anthropic verified connectors from Desktop directory and review permissions during installation.

6

Be careful of using Claude as an add-in to official apps.

Claude can share data across apps (Excel, PowerPoint) without you explicitly directing it. Avoid working with sensitive information while Cowork is active.

Important. While Anthropic enacted measures to reduce risk, you are still responsible for your own use of Claude. Always exercise caution when using Cowork. Report any suspicious behaviour or critical issues to product-team@getmagicea.com.

Magic-specific guidelines

  • Never paste passwords, API keys, or credentials into a Cowork prompt. Don't ask it to access private data or scrape personal information, especially your client's.
  • Never connect a client's email, calendar, or cloud storage without their explicit approval.
  • Never impersonate someone — say "based on their communication style, how would they respond?"
  • Don't modify security settings directly — Claude will refuse.
  • Don't use Claude for clients whose confidentiality clauses cover AI tools — ask your AL first.
  • Aerospace, defense, or government clients: don't connect any client accounts — personal productivity only.
  • Always review everything before it goes to a client. Your sign-off is the last check.

Your responsibility

You remain responsible for all actions Claude takes on your behalf:

  • Any content published or messages sent.
  • Purchases or financial transactions.
  • Data accessed or modified.
  • Actions taken by scheduled tasks running on your behalf.
  • Actions taken through computer use on your desktop and apps.
  • Respecting third-party terms of service, including restrictions on automated access.

Not sure if something is allowed? Ask your Account Lead. They'll check with the Product Team if they don't know.
